# Grade 9 · Fundamentals of Computer and Application — assessment blueprint

**The subject is 100 marks: 50 external and 50 internal.** Gyansetu can prepare
a student for one of those halves and can only support the other. This document
is mostly about keeping that distinction visible, because a study site that
quietly optimises for the written paper teaches a student to neglect half their
grade.

---

## 1 · The two halves

### External — 50 marks · written examination · 2 hours

Set from the CDC specification grid. Questions use revised Bloom's levels:
remembering, understanding, application, and higher ability.

| Unit | Title | External marks | Share |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | Introduction to computer | 6 | 12% |
| 2 | Computer Components | 7 | 14% |
| 3 | Computer software | **9** | **18%** |
| 4 | Memory/Storage Unit | 8 | 16% |
| 5 | Internet and its Application | 8 | 16% |
| 6 | Multimedia | 6 | 12% |
| 7 | Emerging Technology | 6 | 12% |
| | | **50** | **100%** |

The spread is flat — no unit is below 12% or above 18%. There is no unit a
student can safely skip, and no unit worth twice another. **Units 3, 4 and 5
together are 50% of the paper.**

### Internal — 50 marks · awarded by the school

| S.N. | Activity | Marks | Can Gyansetu help? |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Participation — attendance, homework, classwork | 5 | No. This is classroom conduct |
| 2.1 | Practical work — conduction | 15 | **Partly** — it can explain what each activity is for and what good work looks like |
| 2.2 | Practical work — record keeping | 3 | **Partly** — it can show what a record should contain |
| 3.1 | Project work — conduction | 10 | **Partly** — same |
| 3.2 | Project work — record keeping | 2 | **Partly** |
| 4 | Viva on practical and project work | 5 | **Yes** — a viva asks the student to explain their own work, which is what retrieval practice rehearses |
| 5 | Internal exam — two trimesters, 5 + 5 | 10 | **Yes** — same preparation as the external paper |
| | **Total** | **50** | |

**30 of the 50 internal marks are practical and project work**, assessed before
internal and external supervisors, on both the product and the student's skill
with apparatus. No website can earn those for a student. It can make sure they
know what is coming and are not surprised by it.

## 2 · What this means for the product

Four consequences, each already visible in Unit 1.

**The unit chip shows practical hours.** Grade 10 units carry *hrs · marks*;
Grade 9 units carry *theory hrs · marks · practical hrs*. A student reading
"8 hrs · 6 marks" would reasonably conclude the unit is small. It is 18 hours.

**The exam-focus block names what is assessed elsewhere.** Unit 1's says the
10 practical hours are assessed inside the school's internal 50, not in the
written paper. That sentence is the honest version of "exam focus".

**The question bank is weighted to the external grid, and says so.** It is not
weighted to the whole subject, because the internal half is not examined by
multiple choice.

**Practice questions are labelled as authored.** The quiz page states plainly
that these are written for Gyansetu, follow the form the unit is examined with,
and are **not** past CDC or SEE papers. No question anywhere in the product is
presented as official.

## 3 · The balance a question bank must keep

Grade 10 learned this expensively: the OOP bank was built without the grid and
ended up with the second-heaviest unit holding zero questions. The grid for
every Grade 9 unit is already on file, so the target is set before writing.

| Kind | What it tests | Target share |
|---|---|---:|
| Recall and terminology | definitions the paper asks for verbatim | ~35% |
| Conceptual understanding | distinguishing two things that sound alike | ~30% |
| Application | a scenario, and which idea applies to it | ~25% |
| Higher ability | judging, comparing, justifying | ~10% |

Those bands mirror the grid's own Bloom columns, which the document publishes
as whole-paper totals rather than per unit — so they are a target, not a
transcription, and this file says so.

**Per-unit target, once the subject is complete:** roughly 1.2–1.5 questions per
external mark, which is 60–75 questions for 50 marks. Each unit's share should
track its marks, not its topic count — Unit 7 has 9 topics and 6 marks, and
must not end up with the largest bank.

### Where Unit 1 stands

| | |
|---|---|
| Questions | 8 |
| External marks | 6 |
| Per mark | 1.33 — inside the target band |
| Difficulty | 4 easy · 4 medium · 0 hard |
| Topics covered | 8 distinct, across all four official topics |
| Duplicate prompts or ids | 0 |
| Draw | 8 of 8, shuffled |

**Two honest weaknesses, recorded rather than smoothed over.** There are no
*hard* questions yet, and the bank equals the draw — so a retake is the same
eight questions in a different order. Both are consequences of a single unit
being authored. The fix is the same for both: the bank grows past the draw as
Units 2–7 arrive, and the quiz limit stays at 8 until it does.

## 4 · Practical and project work — what the product owes it

Not built yet, and named here so it is not forgotten.

The curriculum gives each unit a practical activity list with its own hours.
These are 30 of 50 internal marks, and Gyansetu currently has nowhere to put
them. A **practical-activity block** is needed: for each unit, the activities
the curriculum names, what a good record of one looks like, and what the viva
is likely to ask about it.

Unit 1's six activities — charts, presentations and a speech competition — are
summarised in the content blueprint but are not yet on the page. That is the
first gap to close for this subject after Unit 2.
