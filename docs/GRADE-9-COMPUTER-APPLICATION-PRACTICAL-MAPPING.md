# Grade 9 · Fundamentals of Computer and Application — practical mapping

**64 theory hours and 64 practical hours. Thirty of the fifty internal marks
are practical and project work.** This document maps every practical activity
the curriculum lists, for all seven units, and says plainly what Gyansetu can
do about each one and what it cannot.

It is a map, not a plan of record. **Unit 1's practical experience is built.
Units 2–7 are mapped and not built**, because Units 2–7 themselves are not
written.

---

## 1 · Where this comes from, and how far it was checked

The practical activity lists are section 5 of the curriculum, *Suggested
Practical and Project Works*, folios 15–17 (PDF pages 20–22 of
`cdc-2078.pdf`, 321 pages).

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Text extraction, method 1 (stream inflate, latin1) | 32 activities, 7 units |
| Text extraction, method 2 (page tree + `/ToUnicode`) | identical |
| **Visual rendering of the pages** | **PDF.js 3.11.174 to canvas, folios 15, 16 and 17 read as images** |
| Hours per unit vs `specification-grid.js` | 10 · 10 · 8 · 6 · 12 · 8 · 10 = **64**, agrees |
| Theory + practical | 64 + 64 = **128 working hours**, agrees with the subject header |

The curriculum calls these activities **samples**: *"the tasks presented here
are the samples only. A teacher can assign the extra practical and project
works as per the students' need or specific context."* That sentence is
quoted on the Unit 1 page, because a student who treats a sample list as a
syllabus will be surprised when their teacher sets something else. **The
hours are not samples** — they are the unit's allocation.

**Two findings worth stating rather than smoothing over.**

**Twenty-one of thirty-two are presentations.** The practical half of this
subject is mostly about explaining theory to an audience, not about operating
a machine. That is a fact about the curriculum, not a complaint about it —
but it changes what a study site owes the practical component. The useful
help is not a simulator; it is knowing what each presentation must cover,
and being able to say the content out loud.

**Exactly one activity is a computer task.** 7.2 asks the student to store
information in cloud storage with their teacher. Everything else produces
paper, slides or speech. A product that built simulations for this list would
be building the wrong thing.

## 3 · What Gyansetu can honestly do

Four levels. The distinction matters, because the brief for this phase is
explicit that a simulation must never be presented as a replacement for a
physical activity — and here, almost nothing could be.

| Level | What it means | Honest about |
|---|---|---|
| **Brief** | The activity is stated on the unit page: what it asks, what you need, which topic it builds on, what to hand in, and what separates good work from work that merely exists | Every activity can have this |
| **Log** | The student's own three-state record — *not done yet / I have done this / done and written up* — kept on their device | It is the student's claim, not the product's knowledge. Nothing is verified, nothing is a mark, nothing is sent anywhere |
| **Rehearse** | An existing drill, figure or question that rehearses the *knowledge* the activity applies | Rehearsing the content of a presentation is not giving the presentation |
| **Cannot** | The physical act: drawing on chart paper, standing up in front of a class, visiting a shop | Named as such, every time |

**No activity in this subject can be completed inside Gyansetu.** Not one.
The product's whole contribution to 30 internal marks is to make sure the
student knows what is expected, has rehearsed the content, and has not thrown
away the record-keeping marks.

### Why `done` and `recorded` are separate states

The curriculum's internal mark distribution separates them:

| | Doing it | Writing it up |
|---|---:|---:|
| Practical work | 15 | **3** |
| Project work | 10 | **2** |

Five marks hang on the write-up. A log that merged the two would teach a
student that finishing the chart is the end of the job.

## 5 · Project work, which is not the same thing

12 of the 50 internal marks are **project work** (10 conduction, 2 record
keeping) and are separate from the 18 for practical work. The curriculum
does not publish a separate project list for this subject: it says *"Project
works should be based on the mentioned lists or created by teachers."*

Two rules it does state, both worth knowing in advance:

- Project work is assessed on **the report and the presentation of it**.
- When a project is presented by a group, **every member submits their own
  short reflection** on the presented report.

The practical examination is conducted **in the presence of internal and
external supervisors**, and assesses both the product of the work and the
student's skill in using apparatus.

Three activities in the list are project-shaped rather than exercise-shaped —
3.4 (the field report), 5.3 (current trends of the Internet in Nepal) and 6.3
(a multimedia presentation on a contemporary topic). They are named here so
that a later phase does not have to re-derive the judgement.

## 6 · What is built, and what is not

**Built, Unit 1 only:**

- Six activities on the page with requirements, expected outcome and what to
  hand in
- A record-keeping block that explains the 3 marks and what a record contains
- Two independent exercises, clearly marked as not from the curriculum
- A persistent three-state log per activity, with a per-unit tally
  (`runtime/practical.js`, `ProgressService.recordPractical`)
- `practicalHours` for all seven units in the specification grid, and the
  unit chip reading *theory hrs · marks · practical hrs*

**Not built:**

- Units 2–7's activity briefs, because Units 2–7 are not written
- The field-report template for 3.4, the strongest single addition available
  to this subject's practical half
- Any revision-page surfacing of practical progress. The log is per unit and
  the revision page does not yet know it exists
- Any export of the log. A student who changes device loses their ticks

**Not claimed:**

- No teacher has reviewed this mapping, in either language
- No student has used the log
- Whether a given school follows the sample list or sets its own work is
  unknown and unknowable from here, which is why the page says so
